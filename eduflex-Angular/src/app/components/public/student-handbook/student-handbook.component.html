01 · Before you arrive

Six weeks out: what to lock in first

  • Confirmation of Enrolment (CoE) issued by your institution — everything else, including your visa, is built on this.
  • Student visa (subclass 500) granted, with your visa grant letter saved as PDF and printed.
  • Overseas Student Health Cover (OSHC) purchased for your full visa duration — it's a visa condition, not optional.
  • Accommodation booked for at least your first 2–4 weeks so you're not house-hunting jet-lagged.
  • Flights booked to arrive a few days before orientation, not the night before.
  • Scans of your passport, visa grant, CoE, OSHC certificate, and transcripts saved to cloud storage, separate from your luggage.
☀

Seasons are reversed. December–February is summer, June–August is winter. A Semester 1 start (February) means arriving in late-summer heat; a Semester 2 start (July) means arriving in winter — pack accordingly.

Hand luggage, not checked bags

  • Passport, visa grant letter, CoE, and OSHC certificate
  • Proof of accommodation for your first nights
  • A small amount of Australian dollars for transport and food on arrival
  • Prescription medication, in original packaging with your name on it

03 · Enrolment & the academic calendar

Know your census date

Typical semester structure

  • Semester 1: late February – June
  • Semester 2: July – November
  • A mid-year and end-of-year break of several weeks
  • Some courses run trimesters instead — check your own calendar

Inside each semester

  • Orientation / O-Week before teaching starts
  • A census date, usually ~3–4 weeks in
  • A mid-semester break (~1 week)
  • A formal exam or assessment period at the end
⚠

The census date is the most important date each semester. Withdraw before it and there's no financial or academic penalty. Withdraw after, and you're generally still billed, and it can appear as a fail. Put every census date in your calendar the day enrolment opens.

Enrolling in units each semester

  1. Check your course structure — core vs elective units, prerequisites — in your course handbook.
  2. Enrol via the student portal before the deadline.
  3. Confirm your study load meets your visa's full-time requirement.
  4. Check your timetable for clashes before census.
04 · Classes, grading & support

What's different from many overseas systems

  • Lectures and tutorials are separate — the tutorial or lab is small, discussion-based, and usually where attendance is actually tracked.
  • Assessment is continuous, not one final exam — expect essays, quizzes, presentations, and group work across the semester.
  • Academic integrity is enforced strictly, including on AI-generated text. When in doubt, cite it.
  • Grades typically run High Distinction / Distinction / Credit / Pass / Fail — exact bands vary by institution.
  • Emailing academic staff is normal — most list consultation hours and reply within a few working days.

Academic support

  • Academic skills / writing centres
  • Peer mentoring and study groups
  • Library research consultations
  • Disability and accessibility services

Personal support

  • Free, confidential counselling
  • International student advisors
  • Student union / guild advocacy
  • Cultural, faith, and LGBTQ+ societies
05 · Culture & everyday life

Settling into a multicultural country

Roughly a third of Australia's residents were born overseas. You will not be the only international student, and you won't be far from food, faith communities, or language from home.

Before European settlement, this land was cared for by hundreds of Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander nations, whose connection to it spans over 60,000 years. You'll hear an "Acknowledgement of Country" at orientation, graduations, and public events — a normal, respectful part of Australian civic life.

Social norms worth knowing early

  • Directness reads as normal, not rude — first names are used quickly, including with lecturers.
  • Tipping isn't expected — hospitality staff earn a legal minimum wage.
  • Punctuality matters for class; social plans are looser.
  • Queuing is taken seriously — wait your turn.
  • Public conversation is kept low-key, especially on transport.

Never wire money, share your banking password, or send a rental deposit before inspecting in person or by video call. Rental and job scams specifically target new arrivals in their first weeks.

07 · Housing

On-campus vs share housing

On-campus / residential colleges

  • Simplest option for your first semester — no lease, bills usually included
  • Built-in social community, closest to classes
  • Apply early; popular colleges fill months ahead

Off-campus share housing

  • Cheaper long-term; listed on realestate.com.au, domain.com.au, flatmates.com.au
  • Requires a lease and a bond (typically 4 weeks' rent, held by a government bond authority)
  • Inspect in person or via live video call before paying anything
✺

Book temporary accommodation for your first two to four weeks rather than signing a 12-month lease sight-unseen. It gives you time to inspect share houses in person once you know your timetable.

Concession fares aren't automatic for international students — eligibility differs by state and sometimes visa subclass. Ask your international student office which concession, if any, applies to you.

Australia drives on the left. You can generally drive on a valid overseas licence for a limited period after arrival — the exact window is set by your state's road authority.

09 · Work rights & jobs

Know your visa's work-hour limit

⚠

Subclass 500 work rights are currently capped at 48 hours per fortnight while your course is in session, uncapped during official breaks. These rules change periodically — confirm the current limit with the Department of Home Affairs or your institution before starting any job.

  • Get your Tax File Number ready — without one, tax is withheld at the top rate.
  • Confirm you'll be paid at least the national minimum wage, with payslips.
  • Check you're being paid superannuation if eligible.
  • The Fair Work Ombudsman investigates underpayment for free and confidentially, regardless of visa status.
10 · Health & safety

Cover, sun, and emergencies

  • OSHC is your primary cover for the length of your visa and is compulsory.
  • Some nationalities have a Reciprocal Health Care Agreement with Australia — check if yours applies.
  • Book a GP for non-urgent issues; save emergency departments for genuine emergencies.
✺

Australia has some of the world's highest UV levels, even on mild days. Slip on a shirt, slop on SPF 30+, slap on a hat, seek shade, slide on sunglasses.

11 · Getting help

Use your support services early

  • International student advisors — visa conditions, OSHC claims, general welfare.
  • Student union / guild — free advocacy for academic and tenancy disputes.
  • Counselling services — confidential, usually free for a set number of sessions.
  • Study Australia and the Department of Home Affairs websites are the authoritative sources for visa condition changes.
